# Cold-call playbook (v3, conversational)

Per-account scripts live in `scripts/accounts/`. They give you the account facts and account-specific questions. This page gives you the conversation: how to open, what to ask, how to respond to what you hear, and how to leave every call with a next step.

**What changed from v2:** v2 was a straight line (opener, five questions, pitch, visit ask). Real calls branch in the first ten seconds: a receptionist answers, the person is busy, they say "send a mail", or they already have an ERP. v3 is built around those branches, and around one habit: **always have the next question ready, and make it about their answer, not your product.**

---

## 1. The goal of a cold call

You are not selling Odoo on the phone. You are trying to leave with **one of these**, in this order of value:

1. A visit or a 30-minute meeting with a date.
2. The right person's name, direct number and a good time to call.
3. One real fact about how they run today (what system, what still lives in Excel, who owns it).
4. Permission to call back on a specific date.

If you got none of these, the call was too short or you talked too much. If you got any one, the call worked.

**Talk-listen rule:** if you have been talking for more than 20 seconds without a question mark, stop and ask something.

## 2. How to sound curious (the five moves)

These are the habits that make a call feel like a conversation instead of a script.

| Move | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Ask the second question** | Never move on after one answer. Ask one follow-up about what they just said. | "Excel mein? Kaunsi sheet sabse badi hai, stock wali ya costing wali?" |
| **Echo** | Repeat their last two or three words as a question. They will explain. | Them: "Thoda manual hai." You: "Thoda manual?" |
| **Ask for the last time** | Ask about a real recent event, not a general opinion. People remember stories, not averages. | "Last time audit hua tha, ek batch ki history nikalne mein kitna time laga?" |
| **Ask who** | Pain lives with a person. Find out who feels it. | "Yeh month-end pe sabse zyada kisko pareshan karta hai: accounts ko ya aapko?" |
| **Label, then ask** | Name what you heard, then check it. | "Lagta hai abhi sab theek chal raha hai, bas teesre plant ke baad thoda tight hoga. Sahi samjha?" |

Useful short follow-ups to keep in your pocket:

- "Matlab?" / "Thoda example de sakte hain?" / "Can you give me an example?"
- "Yeh kitni baar hota hai, har hafte ya month-end pe?"
- "Abhi yeh kaun sambhalta hai?"
- "Agar yeh theek ho jaaye, toh sabse pehle kya badlega?"
- "Yeh hamesha se aisa hai, ya naya plant ya nayi company aane ke baad shuru hua?"

**Don't** ask about revenue, margins or budget on a first call. **Don't** criticise their current software or vendor. **Don't** ask three questions in one breath.

## 3. Language

Same rule as before: open in the account's language (Gujarati by default, Hindi where the script says so), then mirror whatever they answer in. Keep technical words in English: ERP, stock, batch, GST, BOM, costing, go-live. The example lines below are in Hinglish with English versions; the account scripts have Gujarati lines for the key questions.

## 4. The opener (first 15 seconds)

Keep it short and honest. Tell them it's a cold call; it lowers their guard more than pretending it isn't.

> **Hinglish:** Namaste [name] ji, main Dhaval, Brilworks se, Ahmedabad. Seedha bolun toh yeh ek cold call hai. 30 second de sakte hain? Phir aap batana baat karni hai ya nahi.
>
> **English:** Hi [name], this is Dhaval from Brilworks in Ahmedabad. To be upfront, this is a cold call. Can I take 30 seconds, and then you tell me if it's worth continuing?

If they say yes, give the **reason in one line** (the account fact from the script) and go straight to **one easy question**:

> Main aapki website dekh raha tha: [account fact]. Hum manufacturers ke saath ERP ka kaam karte hain, toh ek cheez samajhni thi. Abhi stock aur accounts ke liye kya use karte hain, Tally ya koi ERP?

That question is deliberate. Whatever they answer (Tally, Excel, Zoho, Microsoft, "pata nahi") opens a branch below.

## 5. Branches: what you hear, and what you say next

### A. Receptionist or operator answers

Goal: get the right **name**, the **best time**, and if possible a **direct number or extension**.

> **You:** Namaste, main Dhaval, Brilworks se. Ek chhoti si help chahiye thi. Aapke yahan stock aur accounts ka software kaun dekhta hai: [name from script] ji, ya koi aur?

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Kis baare mein hai?" | "Business software ke baare mein, stock aur accounts ka. Do minute ki baat hai. Woh available hain?" |
| "Sir abhi nahi hain." | "Koi baat nahi. Woh usually kab milte hain, subah ya shaam? ... Aur unka extension ya direct number mil sakta hai, taaki aapko baar baar disturb na karun?" |
| "Mail kar dijiye." | "Zaroor. Kis naam se bhejun, aur unka email kya hai? ... Mail ke baad main Thursday ko call karun toh unse baat ho jayegi?" |
| "Aap mujhe bata dijiye." | Treat them as a helper, not a wall. "Bilkul. Aap bata sakte hain, abhi aapke yahan billing aur stock Tally pe hai ya koi ERP?" One question, then ask for the name. |

Always ask the receptionist's name and use it next time: "Aapka naam? ... Thank you Priya ji, Thursday ko call karta hoon."

### B. Right company, wrong person (IT, accounts staff, a junior)

This person is often the **best source of facts** even if they can't decide. Don't pitch them; ask them.

> **You:** Aap hi sahi insaan ho yeh samajhne ke liye. Abhi aapke yahan ERP ya Tally, kya chal raha hai? ... Iska decision usually kaun leta hai, MD sir ya finance head?
>
> **Then:** Kya aap mujhe unse connect kar sakte hain, ya main seedha call karun toh aapka naam le sakta hoon?

If the IT person says "not required", that is usually a statement about **their** workload, not the owner's priorities. Thank them, note what you learned, and reach the business owner through another route (see branch F).

### C. "Busy hoon" / "Abhi time nahi hai"

Don't push the pitch. Ask for a specific slot, and give one line of why so the callback is warm.

> **You:** Bilkul, samajh sakta hoon. Ek line mein bataun kyon call kiya: [account fact, one line]. Kal 11 baje theek rahega ya shaam 5 ke baad?
>
> **English:** Of course. In one line, I called because [account fact]. Would tomorrow at 11 or after 5 work better?

If they say "no, not interested" while busy, see branch G. If they hang up without a slot, retry once after 7 to 10 days at a different time of day, then switch to email or LinkedIn.

### D. "Email bhej do" / "Send me details"

This is usually a polite exit. Turn it into a **micro-commitment**: one specific question so the email is about them, plus a callback slot.

> **You:** Zaroor bhejta hoon. Taaki generic mail na bheju, ek cheez bata dijiye: aapke liye zyada important kya hai, [topic A from script] ya [topic B]?
>
> **Then:** Aur mail ke baad Friday ko 2 minute call kar lun, ki kaam ka laga ya nahi?
>
> **English:** Happy to. So it isn't a generic email, which matters more for you right now: [topic A] or [topic B]? And can I call you on Friday for two minutes to see if it was useful?

Confirm the email address back letter by letter. Send within the hour, and log the callback date. Use `scripts/02-follow-up-emails.md`.

### E. "Humare paas already ERP hai"

**Never argue and never criticise their system.** A company that already chose an ERP has proved it buys software. Your job is to understand where it stands, so you can come back at the right time with the right angle.

First, always ask these three (in any order):

1. "Kaunsa software hai?" (if they don't know the name: "Koi baat nahi. Woh kisne lagaya tha, koi local vendor ya company ki IT team?")
2. "Kab se chal raha hai?"
3. "Ise kaun own karta hai, matlab koi badlav chahiye toh kisse baat hoti hai?"

Then pick the sub-branch:

**E1. Just bought or implementing (for example, Microsoft Dynamics, signed recently)**

They are excited and busy. Be genuinely interested; this call is about the follow-up date.

> "Achha, congratulations. Kaunsa version, Business Central ya kuch aur? ... Implementation partner kaun hai? ... Go-live kab ka target hai? ... Phase 1 mein kya aa raha hai, aur kya baad mein? Production aur costing bhi pehle phase mein hai?"
>
> **Exit:** "Bahut badhiya. Main go-live ke 2-3 mahine baad ek baar call karun, ki kaisa chal raha hai? Kuch cheezein hoti hain jo pehle phase mein reh jaati hain, unke liye main kaam aa sakta hoon."

Why this works: first-phase scope often leaves gaps (production, QC, costing, a CRM for sales, plant-level reporting). You don't need to win the core ERP; you need to be the name they remember when the gap shows up. Do not claim to implement Microsoft products unless Brilworks actually does.

**E2. Long-running ERP (5+ years, often a local or custom system)**

> "7-8 saal! Toh kaafi customise hua hoga aapke process ke hisaab se. ... Abhi bhi koi report hai jo Excel mein bahar nikaal ke banani padti hai? ... Jo vendor ne lagaya tha, woh abhi bhi support dete hain? ... Naya plant ya nayi company aayi toh woh bhi isi mein add hui?"
>
> **Exit:** "Samajh gaya. Aapke yahan iska owner kaun hai, IT ya accounts head? Unse 10 minute baat ho sakti hai?"

Old systems have predictable pressure points: vendor support thinning out, e-invoicing and GST changes, new units or companies that don't fit, and reports rebuilt in Excel every month. Ask about them; don't assert them.

**E3. Zoho, Tally add-ons or other cloud accounting**

> "Achha, Zoho. Books aur Inventory, ya poora Zoho One? ... Production bhi Zoho mein hota hai, matlab BOM, batch aur QC? Ya woh alag register ya Excel mein? ... Audit mein ek lot ki history kahan se nikalte hain?"
>
> **Exit:** "Theek hai. Agar production side kabhi system mein laana ho toh main kaam aa sakta hoon. Main 2 mahine baad ek baar check karun?"

**E4. Tally plus Excel** (this is the core fit, so dig)

> "Tally accounts ke liye toh best hai. Excel mein sabse badi sheet kaunsi hai: stock, costing ya production planning? ... Usse kaun update karta hai, aur kitni baar? ... Last time stock match nahi hua tha, toh kya hua?"

If they say "Tally is enough": "Bilkul, Tally chalu reh sakta hai accounts aur GST ke liye. Main sirf samajhna chahta tha, [account pain question]?"

**E5. Happy with their ERP, no pain** (for example, "9 saal se chal raha hai, koi problem nahi")

This is a real outcome, not a failure. Don't hunt for pain they say isn't there; that is when a call turns pushy. Your job now is to **leave a good impression, learn three facts and earn permission for one future call.**

1. **Accept it and be curious about why it works.** People enjoy explaining what works.
   > "Bahut badhiya, 9 saal smoothly chalna kam hota hai. Kya cheez sabse achhi lagti hai usmein?"
2. **Ask the wish question.** It surfaces small gaps without calling them problems.
   > "Agar usmein ek cheez add kar sakte, toh kya hoti?" / "Koi report hai jo abhi bhi Excel mein banti hai?"
3. **Ask about timing, not pain.** These are the moments ERPs get reviewed.
   > "Iska AMC ya licence renewal kab hota hai?" / "Agle 1-2 saal mein naya plant, nayi company ya export market plan hai?"
4. **Ask who owns it** (if you don't know): "Isme koi badlav chahiye toh kisse baat hoti hai?"
5. **Close warmly, with a light next touch.**
   > **Hinglish:** "Sunke achha laga ki system itna achha chal raha hai. Main aapka time aur nahi leta. Kya main 6 mahine baad ek baar call kar sakta hoon, bas yeh poochne ke liye ki kuch badla ya nahi? ... Thank you [name] ji."
   >
   > **English:** "Good to hear it's working that well, so I won't take more of your time. Would it be all right if I checked in once in about six months, just to see whether anything has changed? Thanks, [name]."

If they say yes, set status `Nurture` with that date. If they say "no need", thank them and set `Not a fit` (not `Do not contact`, unless they asked you not to call).

**What to log:** the ERP name, how long they've used it, who supports it (vendor, in-house or partner), who owns it, the renewal month if they shared it, and any trigger (new plant, new company, export). That turns a "no" into a dated reason to call back.

**Angles by type of system** (questions only; don't claim anything about their system):

| What they have | Good curious question |
|---|---|
| Local or lesser-known ERP, many years | "Jo company support deti hai, woh abhi bhi active hai? GST ya e-invoice ke changes kitni jaldi aa jaate hain?" |
| Built in-house | "Achha, apni team ne banaya! Isse kaun maintain karta hai? Agar woh insaan chhutti pe ho ya chala jaaye toh kya hota hai?" |
| Oracle, SAP or another large suite | "Kaunsa Oracle, NetSuite ya E-Business Suite? Production aur plant ka kaam bhi usi mein hai, ya sirf accounts aur purchase?" If everything runs on it and they're happy, they are probably not our customer; say thanks and set `Not a fit` or a 12-month `Nurture`. |

### F. "Not required" / "Zaroorat nahi hai"

Respect it, and ask **one** question to learn why. The answer tells you whether to come back.

> **You:** Fair hai. Ek cheez pooch sakta hoon, taaki main aapka time dobara waste na karun: kya isliye ki sab already system mein hai, ya abhi priority kuch aur hai?

| Their reason | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Sab system mein hai." | "Kaunsa system?" Then go to branch E. |
| "Abhi priority nahi." | "Samajh gaya. 6 mahine baad ek baar call karun?" Log the date. |
| "Hum chhote hain, zaroorat nahi." | "Theek hai, thank you." Mark `Not a fit` if it's true. |
| The person isn't the decision maker (for example, IT) | Thank them. Reach the owner through a different route later (LinkedIn, email, or the plant line), with a different angle. Don't go over their head the same week. |

### G. A clear "no" or "don't call again"

> "Bilkul, sorry for disturbing. Thank you."

Set status to `Do not contact`. Do not follow up on any channel. A busy "abhi nahi" is not a clear no; "please don't call us again" is.

### H. No answer, number not reachable, number not in service

| What happened | Next step |
|---|---|
| Rings, no answer | Retry at a different time of day. Up to **3 attempts across 7 days** (for example, 10:30, 15:30, 18:00), then switch channel. |
| Switched off or not reachable | Same as above. Mobiles are often off during plant hours; try after 18:00. |
| **Number not in service** | Mark `Bad number`. Before calling again: (1) try the other listed numbers, (2) check the website contact page and Google Maps listing again, (3) IndiaMART or TradeIndia listing, (4) for listed companies, the company secretary or investor-relations contact in their stock-exchange filings or annual report, (5) LinkedIn for the named director. Write what you found in the account notes. |

## 6. When you hear real pain

When they describe a manual step, a delay or a mismatch, **slow down**. Ask one more question about it (move: "ask the second question"), then reflect it back:

> "Toh agar main sahi samjha: [their words]. Aur yeh [month-end / har audit / naya plant] ke time aur badh jaata hai."

Only now give **one** line of value, from the account script, and ask for the visit:

> "Hum yahi kaam karte hain, [value line]. Kya main next week 30 minute aa ke dikha sakta hoon ki aapke setup mein yeh kaisa dikhega? Tuesday ya Thursday?"

## 7. Clean exits that earn the next call

Every call ends with **something specific**: a date, a name or a number. Never end with "theek hai, main contact karta hoon."

| Situation | Exit line |
|---|---|
| Meeting agreed | "Main abhi WhatsApp pe confirm bhejta hoon. Yeh number sahi hai?" |
| Sending an email | "Mail aaj hi bhejta hoon, subject mein '[company] + [topic]' hoga. Friday ko call karta hoon." |
| Referred to someone | "Unka number mil sakta hai? Aur main unhe aapka naam le ke call karun?" |
| On another ERP | "Main [month] mein ek baar check karun, go-live ke baad kaisa chal raha hai?" |
| Not now | "Theek hai, [month] mein ek baar call karun?" |
| Every call | Thank them by name. "Bahut shukriya [name] ji, aapka time dene ke liye." |

## 8. Objections, quick reference

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Tally is enough." | "Tally accounts aur GST ke liye bahut achha hai, woh chalu rahega. Stock across plants, batch costing aur approvals abhi kaise handle hote hain?" |
| "Too costly." | "Phase 1 (inventory, purchase, sales, accounts with GST) ₹2 lakh se shuru hota hai. Per-user licence nahi chahiye toh ERPNext bhi option hai. Exact number aapka setup dekh ke hi bata paunga." |
| "We tried an ERP before and it failed." | "Kya hua tha? ... Hum phase mein go-live karte hain, toh day one pe kuch nahi tootta." |
| "We use Tranzact or a Tally add-on." | "Achha. Uske baad bhi kya Excel ya WhatsApp pe reh jaata hai?" |
| "Who else uses it?" | "MP mein humne ek building-materials manufacturer ka poora ERP banaya hai. Ahmedabad mein is saal 2-3 plants ke saath reference terms pe shuru kar rahe hain." Never name the MP client. |
| "After March." | "1 April go-live ke liye achha time hai. Uske liye January mein scoping shuru karni padegi. Usse pehle ek baar mil lein?" |
| "We just bought Microsoft / SAP / Zoho." | Branch E1 or E3. Get the follow-up date. |

## 9. Logging

All logs are CSV files in `gtm/logs/`. One row per call, including no answer.

`calls.csv` columns: `date_ist` (`YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`, or `YYYY-MM-DD` if the time wasn't noted), `account_id`, `company`, `caller`, `person_spoken_to`, `role`, `language_used`, `outcome`, `pain_heard`, `objection`, `next_step`, `next_step_date`, `notes`.

Use one of these `outcome` codes so we can count them:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `no_answer` | Rang, no one picked up, or switched off |
| `bad_number` | Number not in service or wrong company |
| `gatekeeper_callback` | Reached reception; got a name or time |
| `gatekeeper_email` | Asked to email instead |
| `referred` | Passed to a named colleague |
| `busy_declined` | Picked up, too busy, no slot given |
| `has_erp` | Already on an ERP (write which one in `notes`) |
| `not_required` | Said no need (write who said it) |
| `spoke_dm` | Real conversation with a decision maker |
| `meeting_booked` | Date agreed |
| `do_not_contact` | Clear no |

Then update `status`, `next_step` and `next_step_date` in `leads/accounts.csv`. Statuses: Researched, Called - no answer, Called - gatekeeper, Called - spoke to DM, Bad number, Nurture, Meeting booked, Visited, Discovery done, Proposal sent, Won, Lost, Not a fit, Do not contact.

`Nurture` means "not now, but worth a timed follow-up" (for example, they just bought another ERP). Always give a `Nurture` account a `next_step_date` and an angle in `next_step`.

Run `python3 gtm/tools/build.py` afterwards. It refreshes the Excel view and rewrites each account script, including a "Where we stand" section built from `calls.csv`.

Seventeen calls is still a small sample, but the pattern is now strong enough to act on:

1. **7 of the 12 calls where someone picked up were already on an ERP.** Our Tier A score rewards multi-plant, multi-company, export and hiring signals, which are also the companies most likely to have bought already. Next batch: mix in Tier B single-plant accounts and compare the share still on Tally and Excel. If it is clearly higher, re-weight the score.
2. **Ask "Tally or ERP?" in the first 30 seconds.** It sorts the call into a branch quickly, and an ERP answer is still useful data (name, years, owner, renewal).
3. **5 of 17 never reached a person.** Board lines from websites are stale for some companies. Check numbers on Google Maps and IndiaMART before the next batch, and prefer listed mobiles.
4. **The only warm lead (JEE Pumps) was on Tally and Excel.** That is the profile to find more of.

## Rules

- No email, DM or sequence goes out until it is approved. The drafts in each script are drafts.
- Check names and roles before every call. One LinkedIn result named a director whom the company's own site lists as late.
- Never name the MP client in any message, file or call.
- If someone gives a clear no, set status to `Do not contact` and stop.
